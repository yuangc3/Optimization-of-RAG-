Lightweight Adaptive Top-K Retrieval in FlashRAG
Yidong Zhang and Yuang Cai | CS598APE final project | November 13, 2026
Abstract
We implement and evaluate training-free Adaptive Top-K retrieval using similarity thresholds and adjacent score gaps in
FlashRAG. The goal is to reduce context-processing cost while maintaining answer quality. Early aggressive pruning
reduced context but lost correct answers. We therefore introduce minimum-k and maximum-k guards, a weak-similarity
fallback, and development-only calibration that preserves every observed fixed-5 answer-string hit. We exclude all earlier
questions, calibrate on 200 development questions, freeze two policies, and evaluate six methods on 500 new questions with
three timing repetitions each. The report separates Adaptive-K findings from supporting generation-system speedups and
reports negative results. The experiments use a limited 15,000-document general-knowledge corpus; answer-hit measures are
lexical proxies, and full-Wikipedia benchmark performance is not claimed.
1. Problem, scope, and related work
A fixed retrieval budget can provide unnecessary or insufficient context for individual questions. We study whether scores
already produced by a dense retriever provide a cheap signal for selecting a document prefix. The primary contribution is a
modular implementation and reproducible cost/quality evaluation of two lightweight selection rules; no additional selector
model is trained and inference makes no LLM-feedback calls.
RAG combines external retrieval with generation [1]. Self-RAG learns retrieval and reflection behavior [2]; Adaptive-RAG
learns to select retrieval strategies based on question complexity [3]; DynamicRAG learns dynamic reranking using
generation-quality feedback [4]. Our scope is narrower: deterministic, score-only prefix selection within the FlashRAG
toolkit [5]. We do not reproduce these systems, train comparable models, claim algorithmic novelty for standard
threshold/gap heuristics, or claim state-of-the-art results.
The implementation first retrieves a bounded candidate pool and then chooses the number of documents sent to the
generator. It does not terminate the FAISS distance scan early. Consequently, context reduction and candidate-search
acceleration are separate hypotheses. A successful system-throughput optimization does not establish that the adaptive
selection rule is better.
2. Implementation and optimization decisions
Optimization 1: Lightweight Adaptive Top-K selection (primary)
For score-gap selection, let ranked similarity scores be s1 >= s2 >= ... >= sM. Compute adjacent gaps g_i = s_i - s_(i+1),
consider boundaries between min_k and M-1, and cut after the largest gap if it reaches a calibrated threshold. Otherwise
retain fallback_k. The frozen final policy uses min_k=2, max_k=5, fallback_k=5, and gap threshold=0.03. Ties choose the
earliest allowed boundary. Both score directions are supported and tested, although performance experiments use
inner-product similarities.
The similarity-threshold rule counts the ranked candidates meeting an absolute threshold, clamps the result to the
minimum/maximum budget, and uses fallback_k if no score qualifies. The weak-query fallback avoids interpreting low
similarity as evidence that very little context is sufficient. Its frozen threshold is 0.717440844, with min_k=2, max_k=5, and
fallback_k=5. Scores are not calibrated correctness probabilities; this numeric threshold is specific to the E5/index
configuration.
Both rules validate finite scores, ranking direction, and k bounds. Empty retrieval yields k=0; short result lists retain
available documents. Runtime selection receives scores only, with no access to gold answers. Selection is O(M) per query
with small bounded M. Both policies plug into SequentialPipeline through the same retrieval_policy interface and record the
selected k and reason. The final max_k=5 cap avoids expanding beyond the fixed-5 context budget; fixed-10 remains a
separate reference.

3. Experimental protocol
Data separation and calibration
The source is the pinned FlashRAG NQ JSONL, revision bcafb8dd07d453be3cbeeeb3f78be1841bddf92c, containing 3,610
questions. We exclude the prior 517 unique questions, sample using seed 598, and allocate 200 development plus 500
evaluation questions with no normalized-question or ID overlap. These are project-specific splits drawn from the official test
JSONL, not the official NQ development split. IDs and SHA-256 values are preserved in split_manifest.json.
Calibration considers 81 configurations across the two policy families. The criterion is zero lost individual development
answer-string hits relative to fixed-5, then minimum mean k; ties prefer a larger minimum budget and canonical
configuration order. It uses development retrieval results and gold aliases only. No LLM answer or final-test answer is used
to select the policies. The chosen rules are frozen before development generation and final evaluation. Calibration retains
24/200 development hits; mean k becomes 4.945 for score-gap and 4.770 for threshold.
The frozen primary answer-quality check compares observed EM and F1 against fixed-5; all other metrics and methods are
reported regardless of outcome. No policy is retuned or selected using the final results. Maintaining development lexical
coverage does not guarantee answer quality.

Timing and metrics
Each method receives an eight-question warmup outside timing. All six methods run for three measured repetitions, with
method order rotated and reversed according to a fixed schedule. CUDA synchronization encloses the complete inference
interval. Search, selection, prompt construction, generation, process CPU time, and peak allocated/reserved GPU memory
are recorded. Evaluation and token counting run outside inference timing. No profiler or forward hooks are active in final
timed runs. Memory figures include both loaded neural models; host peak RSS and minimum hardware requirements were
not measured.
Answer quality uses repository-normalized EM, token F1, and answer-containing accuracy. Retrieval answer hit tests
whether a nonempty normalized gold alias occurs at token boundaries in any selected document. This is a lexical proxy; gold
document relevance labels are unavailable. Context tokens use the actual generator tokenizer after its 1,024-token truncation.
Padded input/output slots are separately recorded and are not useful-token counts. Throughput is total questions divided by
median batch completion time, not inverse single-request latency.
There are 9,000 final generations but only 500 independent final questions. Paired quality intervals use 2,000 question
bootstrap resamples, seed 598. They describe uncertainty on these questions, not timing variance or formal equivalence;
sparse binary outcomes and degenerate zero-change intervals require caution.

Fixed k=3 must be retained in the interpretation: its observed EM is 4.00% and F1 is 8.662%, compared with fixed-5 EM
2.60% and F1 7.661%. Reducing context relative to fixed-5 does not establish that an adaptive method dominates every
fixed-k operating point. The tables report the complete trade-off rather than choosing only a favorable comparator.
The contribution is a working score-only adaptive implementation, guarded development calibration, and a fully reported
evaluation. Candidate-search acceleration and general quality-preserving latency improvements are not presumed from
context reduction. Existing defaults remain unchanged, and calibrated policies are explicit experiment configurations rather
than universal replacements for fixed-k.
5. CPU/GPU characterization and remaining bottlenecks
Earlier profiling established that generation dominates full RAG time; score selection accounts for milliseconds per 500
questions. Controlled CPU/CUDA traces showed many small launches and host-side generation activity. A
fixed-eight-output-token diagnostic reduced kernel count from 39,116 at sorted batch 8 to 10,115 at batch 32, but kernel
duration increased from 467.403 to 813.181 ms because padding increased. A lower wall time does not imply less GPU
computation. Synchronization call counts also fell while host synchronization intervals increased. These separate diagnostic
captures support launch/batching hypotheses, not exact attribution of unprofiled runtime or SM occupancy.
Windows counters on a separate 500-question workload measured median process CPU 38.97 s at batch 8 and 15.02 s at
batch 32, concentrated in the main thread. A separate 32-prompt cProfile run with GetThreadTimes identified linear/attention
entry points, generation-loop completion checks, and module dispatch. CPU time at a CUDA entry point includes host/driver
behavior, not GPU kernel execution; busy waiting can consume CPU. Cumulative function times overlap. No
RMSNorm/RoPE fusion, OpenMP C++ implementation, or -O3 compilation experiment was performed.
WPR native CPU-stack capture was attempted normally and through tool escalation, but Windows rejected the profiling
privilege with error 0xc5585011. A tested administrator-only capture entry point is supplied; actual WPR ETL capture and
native wait-cause analysis remain incomplete. This restriction is stated rather than substituting Python profiling for a
successful WPR capture.
6. Testing, artifact layout, and reproduction
The complete local discovery suite passed 56 tests, with no skipped or failed tests. It covers score direction, threshold
inclusivity, bounds, ties, empty/short inputs, nonfinite and unsorted scores, policy configuration, pipeline integration, FAISS
result handling/cache behavior, document metrics, dataset handling, generator scores/text/dictionaries, batching order and
padding, CPU trace interval accounting, WPR session ownership/cleanup, development/evaluation separation, and
calibration safeguards. Small real Hugging Face generation tests run on CPU without downloading weights. The initial two
warning-test errors were caused by unittest scanning lazy optional vision imports; the test now captures and checks the actual
retrieval warning without importing unrelated vision modules. The initial failure log and final passing log are retained.
Larger-program validation checks all six methods, all three final repetitions, all 500 IDs in original order, selected-document
counts and adaptive prefixes against fixed-5, frozen hashes, and repeated prediction stability. Prediction disagreement counts
are {"fixed_1": 0, "fixed_10": 0, "fixed_3": 0, "fixed_5": 0, "score_gap": 0, "similarity_threshold": 0}. Development
generation adds 1,200 calls across six methods. This is 10,200 new generation evaluations on 700 distinct questions; it is not
a claim of 10,200 independent examples.
No C/C++ source was authored for this project. Valgrind: not applicable to authored Python code; not run.
PyTorch/FAISS contain third-party native implementations, for which no Valgrind-clean claim is made. Optional UI,
training, multimodal, vLLM, multi-GPU, and non-Windows final workloads are outside tested scope.

7. Limitations and project completion status
Implementation, integration, testing, development calibration, fresh-question fixed/adaptive comparisons, profiling evidence,
documentation, and PDF export are delivered. Outcomes are empirical: an implementation can be complete while its
efficiency hypothesis remains partly unsupported. The experiments do not establish universal latency improvement, semantic
quality equivalence, or faster FAISS distance scans. Low corpus answer coverage, lexical relevance proxies, input
truncation, natural variable output lengths, one small model/GPU, three repetitions, other desktop applications, unlocked
clocks, and lack of independent deployment traffic limit generalization. CPU peak memory and minimum hardware were not
measured. WPR native stacks remain blocked by administrator privileges.
The final policies are specific to the recorded retriever and corpus; absolute thresholds need recalibration after resource
changes. A new corpus or policy tuned after inspecting final results requires another unused evaluation set. Future work
should prioritize better corpus coverage, token-budget-aware selection calibrated on development data, and comparison with
strong fixed-k frontiers before additional model-internal kernel optimization.

8. Conclusion
We deliver a modular, tested implementation of similarity-threshold and score-gap Adaptive Top-K with guarded selection
and reproducible development/final data separation. The final tables quantify context, quality, latency, throughput, and
memory together. Supporting system experiments demonstrate substantial generation-throughput improvements, while the
Adaptive-K results must be interpreted according to their own smaller context/quality trade-offs. The report retains
unfavorable outcomes and explicitly identifies unachieved or unmeasured objectives.
